I have been using `antigravity-cli` and Claude Code in my research for a few months now. I have been thinking about how I should use these tools. The general trend I am seeing on X and in paper reviews is towards automating as much as possible. But this seems wrong to me. Here I present four arguments for staying in the loop.

## AI is still bad at some parts of the research process

There are several parts of AI research: (i) exploring the literature and finding gaps, (ii) picking research questions to tackle, (iii) designing experiments, (iv) writing code to implement those experiments, (v) analysing results and drawing conclusions, and (vi) presenting the results and conclusions to others. While AI is great at increasing productivity and quality, it is still terrible at some of them. The following are some good examples in my opinion:

- I have yet to find a tool that does a proper literature search and selection. Current tools are good for finding many papers quickly, but they will miss important works and select useless ones.
- Its taste in research questions and directions can diverge from human interests.
- The code it produces is not optimised for human understanding and is difficult to maintain. It doesn't necessarily follow good patterns or the simplest/most concise implementation. It sometimes introduces error and edge-case handling silently without you making the decision. This makes staying in control of the codebase and taking responsibility for it very difficult.
- It struggles to structure papers in a way that is understandable and compelling; not to mention coding agents' writing style, which is unreadable.

Spending some (human) time in these areas can improve a paper greatly, in my opinion.

## The work is your mental gym

If you stop using your brain altogether, you are going to become less and less intelligent. I even find myself going back to competitive programming problems when I don't have anything to do at work. Why let AI do my job and turn to other activities for mental stimulation? If AI is better than you at some tasks right now, you can always ask AI to review your work afterwards. The point is to stay in shape and improve.

## The effortful parts are the ones that provide true enjoyment and fulfilment

To me, thinking, coding and crafting the paper were always the fun parts, and outsourcing them sucked the joy and the fulfilment out of the job. I miss the feeling of time flying by in a long coding session. And I believe I am not alone in this.

## The research process is still bottlenecked by GPU time

The previous arguments are rendered pointless if you have to compete in an academic race with people automating everything. But I don't believe these people are far ahead in productivity. The main bottleneck for me has always been GPU time, and much of my time is spent waiting for experiments. Why not use that time doing intellectual work, reaping the benefits described in the previous arguments, and potentially improving the final output quality? Of course, this only applies to research areas with such a bottleneck.
